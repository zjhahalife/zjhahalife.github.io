Do superheroes also end up buying raw meat hamburgers while fighting against insect criminals?

2025
Medium
Experimental video and installation
Video duration
4 min 10 sec
Production date
December 3, 2025

Watch video

“Do Superheroes Also End Up Buying Raw-Meat Hamburgers While Fighting Against Insect Criminals?” takes the form of an experimental video work. By placing the symbolic treatment of “insects” and “raw-meat hamburgers” at the center of its narrative trajectory, the piece adopts a deliberately flattened mode of storytelling that challenges the single-directional structures common in previous video practices.

Earlier video and installation works were often constructed around clearly defined conceptual frameworks, such as themes of “alienated labor” or “cultural fusion.” Although figures such as “superheroes” or fast-food motifs—symbols with cultural exteriority— appeared within these works, editing strategies and narrative structures were frequently used to soften their strong semiotic direction, shifting the viewer’s attention toward the underlying systemic relations. Building upon this lineage, the current work employs a de-structured strategy, allowing greater formal freedom within the internal relationships of images and objects. The installation no longer functions as a mere extension of the video but expands into a linguistic and contextual counterpart, forming two parallel “stages” that operate alongside each other.

This loosened narrative model also contains elements that invite further examination. The flattened structure may reduce emotional anchoring for the viewer and produce a sense of disorientation in the absence of a single central story. Yet such instability is also integral to the work’s logic, pointing toward a visual condition where comic aesthetics, televisual formats, and real life continuously intersect. Within this decentralized structure, rhythm, subtle cues, or repetition may further strengthen the internal tension of the piece.

The symbolic elements—“insects,” “raw-meat hamburgers,” “superheroes,” and “farm tools”—maintain a deliberately open and loosely connected relationship. This looseness does not indicate the absence of meaning; rather, it creates an echoing field in which viewers generate their own interpretive connections. The ambiguity and sudden appearance of insects, as well as the superhero as a highly industrialized and consumable body image, both gesture toward a broader theme within contemporary visual culture: the condition of “life forms subjected to being seen.”

The dual-stage structure represents a central formal attempt. Although the two stages currently operate in parallel and their intertextual relationship has not yet reached a state of intense collision, this gap also points toward future potential: by amplifying the tension, contrast, or mutual revelation between video and installation, the work’s sense of “structural absurdity” may become even more pronounced.

In the use of absurdity, the visual strategies within the piece may at times be read as humorous or eccentric rather than critical. Here, absurdity and symbolism function more like an external surface, while the underlying operative logic continues to engage with critiques of visual structure, systems of spectatorship, and narrative organization.

Overall, the work does not seek to construct a single linear narrative. Instead, it positions the viewer within a state of partial legibility, where the elements—“insects,” “raw-meat hamburgers,” “superheroes”—do not act as traditional symbols but as visual disruptions that unsettle habitual modes of looking. The piece establishes a gray zone between comics, television shows, and lived reality, using absurdity as a method for observing and reinterpreting the real.

As a structural experiment in moving-image practice, the work opens new possibilities for the development of future video languages.
