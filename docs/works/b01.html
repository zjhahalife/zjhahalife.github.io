In the context of a foreign land, I employ common and inexpensive fruits as protagonists—objects that transcend geographical and political boundaries yet acquire distinct symbolic meanings in different cultural contexts. Within this body of work, the banana is not merely an object of consumption but also a social, economic, and political signifier. Its production, circulation, and consumption are tied to capitalist commodity systems, global trade histories, and colonial legacies. Thus, it is not only a material entity but also a medium through which an individual’s cultural identity is redefined in the flux of geographic displacement.

In its presentation, I employ models to embody the banana as a symbol, integrating it into real-world geopolitical environments. Against various urban backdrops, the banana may appear humorous, absurd, or provoke reflections on identity. As the geographic context shifts, so too does its meaning—it can serve as a lighthearted reference to cultural consumption or subtly imply the fluidity of identity. Here, photography is not only a static visual archive but also a dynamic intervention—it captures the teleperceptive effect of the banana across different cultural landscapes, documenting how it is reinterpreted, accepted, or misread within new systems of viewing.

Book production

This is a butterfly-bound hardcover book with exposed spine and cloth wrapping. The finished size is 295×420mm (portrait). The front and back covers use lemon yellow cloth with debossed details. The book has 130 pages, using 157 gsm matte art paper, duplex mounted, with a total print run of 300 copies.
