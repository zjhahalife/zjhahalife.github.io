one apple one banana: The Manifestation of Identity in Flux

2025
Medium
Photography

Identity is not fixed but is observed, shaped, and reconstructed within different cultural systems. Through the intervention of hands, the symbolism of color, and the modification of fruit surfaces, this work explores how individuals are encoded or reshaped in the flux of cross-cultural movement.

The banana reveals five-pointed stars through oxidation, symbolizing marks that become visible only when the environment changes, hinting at the latent and temporal nature of identity. The apple is hollowed out, exposing its vulnerability, while the barcode covering it signifies how consumer systems shape both objects and individuals. The varying nail colors reinforce this process — symbols of gender, class, or personality in some cultures, while mere decoration in others. These acts transform fruit beyond mere consumables, turning them into metaphors for identity and cultural encoding.

The photographic medium here is not just a tool for documentation but also one for observation and deconstruction. As Susan Sontag discusses in On Photography, images do not merely reproduce reality but assign new meanings through the act of viewing. In navigating between two cultures, how is the individual recognized? Are they inscribed, or are they consumed?
