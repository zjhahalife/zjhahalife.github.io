Ring-shaped and knotted foods

2025
Medium
Bread, vacuum sealing apparatus, plastic bags, single-channel video, 30 fps, 480p
Video duration
12 min 50 sec

Watch video

Ring-shaped and knotted foods appear across diverse culinary traditions and cultural contexts. From bagels and pretzels to braided pastries, these self-enclosing forms recur across geographical and historical boundaries. As a visual structure, the loop suggests continuity, connection, and repetition while resisting a clearly defined beginning or end. In this sense, the work resonates with Gilles Deleuze’s notion of becoming, in which meaning emerges not from fixed identities but through ongoing processes of transformation.

The project takes two ring-shaped foods as its material and subjects them to a sequence of actions: chewing, regurgitation, reshaping, and reheating. Through these interventions, the foods are displaced from their original cultural and commercial functions and enter an ambiguous state. They oscillate between food and waste, consumption and production, bodily residue and sculptural object.

One section of the work is titled Universal Alchemy. Here, the microwave functions not merely as a kitchen appliance but as a quotidian technological apparatus. Within the routines of student life, the microwave and electric kettle often operate as universal tools capable of transforming nearly any substance into an edible form. Their role recalls Michel de Certeau’s observations on everyday practices, through which ordinary users continually reinterpret and repurpose existing systems.

By bringing together bodily digestion, technological mediation, and material reconfiguration, the work examines transformation as an ongoing condition rather than a discrete event. Food becomes more than an object of consumption; it acts as a medium through which relationships among the body, technology, and cultural memory are negotiated. The circular form remains central throughout—a recurring motif that points toward cycles of ingestion, alteration, expulsion, and renewal, through which matter is continuously assigned new meanings.
