Timber Warfront

2025
Medium
Bandsaw-cut wood, hand-carved wooden stakes, plastic toy soldiers (found objects), printed paper, 502 instant glue, wood glue
Dimensions
Irregular dimensions

Scroll to view 2 images

The work constructs a small-scale territorial model that examines how mobility is shaped, confined, and imagined across multiple geographies. Using bandsaw-cut wood panels layered into a hybrid map, the piece compresses two spatial systems—one associated with where I come from, and one tied to where I currently live. Their boundaries are not meant to fuse seamlessly; instead, they form an unstable surface where territorial logics collide and reconfigure.

Hand-carved wooden stakes, printed paper, nails, and plastic toy figures—objects typically used to mark occupation, strategy, or play—are placed across this landscape. Their fixed positions point to the friction between the freedom of movement we assume and the infrastructural, political, and administrative constraints that continually organize that movement. The toy soldiers in particular operate as small indexes of how occupation becomes aestheticized, gamified, or naturalized within everyday cultural materials.

Rather than staging a literal conflict, the work considers how contemporary mobility often takes on the structure of a managed crossing: regulated, conditional, and uneven. It treats the landscape not as a battlefield but as a diagram of negotiated passages—points where borders, permissions, and trajectories must be repeatedly re-established.

This inquiry extends beyond personal geography. It gestures toward broader discussions on land governance, systems of planetary circulation, and the ways in which certain bodies are enabled to move while others remain structurally peripheral. These concerns intersect with Indigenous conceptions of land as relation rather than possession, emphasizing spatial continuity and responsibility rather than extraction or control. In that sense, the work approaches territory less as a fixed shape than as an ongoing set of relations, continually redrawn through movement, restriction, and imagined access.

As the project continues to develop, it will expand into a larger system-based investigation that connects individual mobility to wider spatial infrastructures. Future iterations will examine how circulation operates across scales—from the intimate routes of daily life to continental corridors, atmospheric systems, and geopolitical arrangements. This expansion will not overwrite the current form but situates it as one component within a broader framework that studies how territories emerge, shift, and negotiate meaning through movement.
