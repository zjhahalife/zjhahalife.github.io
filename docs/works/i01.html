Ambiguous Cartography

2026
Medium
Plaster (cast, carved, and reshaped form), 3D scanning and digital modeling, AI-based image synthesis, hybrid cartographic compositions, inkjet prints, metal needle, bodily interaction (finger)

Scroll to view 3 images

Totem → Memory → Map → Question

I began this work from an uncertainty around the idea of a “spiritual totem,” not as a fixed cultural symbol, but as something unstable and constantly shifting between different environments.

This uncertainty comes from my own experience of moving between cultures, where a sense of belonging is never fully stable or singular.

In my earlier work Timber Warfront, I used a map-like wooden structure combined with toy soldiers to construct a space of conflict.

In that piece, the map was not a neutral representation, but a field where different cultural understandings collide, like stones striking each other in the mind.

This work established my interest in using maps not to describe space, but to expose tension between experiences.

This project was influenced by Jorge Luis Borges and his short text On Exactitude in Science.

In the story, a map becomes so accurate that it matches the empire exactly, and therefore loses its function.

Following this idea, I chose to move in the opposite direction: instead of increasing accuracy, I intentionally produce ambiguity.

Material Process: Plaster Transformation

The work begins with a buffalo as a totemic form, which I sculpt using plaster.

During the making process, I gradually remove its figurative clarity, allowing the form to shift toward something closer to a map-like structure.

Plaster here functions as a transitional material, carrying both physical presence and abstract transformation.
